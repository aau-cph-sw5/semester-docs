# Code of conduct

This semester puts around sixty students into teams they did not choose, working
on a shared codebase with people they have to review and be reviewed by. That
arrangement produces friction by design. This document is about the difference
between the friction that teaches something and the friction that does not.

The Educado cohort adopted the [Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/),
which remains the reference text and is worth reading once. What follows is
specific to this semester and to how it is assessed.

---

## What is expected

Treat the code critically and the person generously. Every pull request on this
semester is reviewed by someone from another team, often someone you have not met.
The review is about whether the code will be workable in two months, never about
whether the author is any good.

Assume the other team is competent and busy. When another team's work blocks yours,
the first move is to ask them, not to work around it silently and not to raise it
first at a review with the partner present.

Give people the information they need to help you. "It does not work" costs a
reviewer twenty minutes of guessing. What you did, what you expected, what
happened, costs you one line.

Say when you are stuck. Two hours is a reasonable time to struggle alone. Two days,
in a two-week sprint, is a fifth of the sprint spent by one person on something
someone else could have unblocked over coffee.

Say when you are wrong. Retracting a review comment or admitting an approach was a
dead end is not a loss of face. Defending a bad decision because you made it costs
the team a sprint.

## What is not acceptable

Harassment, discrimination or demeaning behaviour of any kind, in any channel, in
either language. This includes the informal channels the teams set up themselves.

Personal criticism dressed as code review. There is a real difference between "this
function does three things and will be hard to change" and "this is sloppy work".
The first is useful and the second is not, and everyone can tell which they have
received.

Dismissing someone on the basis of experience. The people who ask the most naive
questions about the domain are frequently the ones who find the assumption
everybody else stopped seeing.

Taking credit for shared work, or leaving a team member's name off something they
contributed to.

Letting a teammate carry your share quietly. This one is worth naming explicitly
because it is the most common problem in group project work and the one people are
most reluctant to raise. It is not disloyal to say that the distribution of work
has become unfair. Leaving it until the assessment is worse for everyone,
including the person carrying too little.

## Professional conduct toward Metro Service

Metro Service colleagues attend the sprint reviews and give their time between
them. They are practitioners with operational jobs, not teaching assistants.

Be prepared. Turn up to a review having read your own backlog. A question already
answered in the material spends their goodwill.

Be honest about what is not finished. Metro would far rather see three things that
work and hear plainly about the fourth than see a demonstration that quietly avoids
what broke.

Respect the confidentiality obligations in
[10-data-handling.md](10-data-handling.md). Their material stays inside the
project.

## If something goes wrong

Raise it early, at the lowest level that can resolve it.

Within the team, with the person, if you can. Most problems are misreadings and
dissolve in a five-minute conversation.

With your supervisor, if you cannot, or if it concerns the distribution of work,
or if a conversation has already been tried.

With the semester coordinator or the Product Owner, for anything spanning teams,
anything involving the partner, or anything you do not want to raise with your
supervisor.

Through the study board's formal channels, for anything involving harassment or
discrimination. Those routes exist, they are independent of the teaching staff,
and using them is not an escalation you need to justify to anyone.

Raising a concern in good faith will never count against you. Retaliation against
someone who raises one is itself a breach of this document.
